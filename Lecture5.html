Choosing k: look for the bend

Tightness (total squared distance to the centres) for k = 1 to 6, best of 12 random starts. Adding groups always makes it smaller. The useful k is where the curve bends and extra groups stop helping much: 2 for the six motors, 3 for the factory.

Distances from Q, nearest first

Your eye stretches each axis to fill the chart, so Q looks close to the failed motors. The algorithm does not. In raw units a gap of 20 degrees swamps any gap in vibration. Rescaling fixes that, but one bad reading can wreck the rescaling, which is why cleaning comes first (slide 12). The voting rule is k nearest neighbours, one of the ten algorithms on slide 11.

Hierarchical clustering: keep merging the closest

Eight motors: the six from the lecture, G at (7, 7) and H at (4.5, 4.5). Every motor starts as its own group. Each step merges the two closest groups. Colours show the groups at the cut you choose.

Controls

The tree of merges (dendrogram)

Height is the distance at which two groups merged. A long vertical gap means a natural place to cut. No k is chosen in advance: you choose the cut afterwards.

Density-based clustering: crowds and loners

A ring of motors around a tight cluster, plus a few strays. A group is any crowded region. Points with no crowd nearby are marked as noise instead of being forced into a group.

core point (crowded)edge point× noise

Controls

Result

k-means expects round groups, so it cuts the ring in half (slide 28). Density-based clustering follows the shape of the crowd and leaves the strays out. The price: you must choose the radius and the crowd size, and a bad choice merges everything or calls everything noise.

From groups to predictions

Ninety factory motors, none labelled. Find the groups, ask an engineer about one motor in each group, then use the named groups to predict. Drag motor G anywhere.

The neuron on the first tab needed a label on every training motor. Here three expert judgements name ninety motors, and anything far from every group is flagged for inspection. This is the route on slides 30 and 31, and the reason clustering matters when labels are expensive.
